SYSTEM / TASK

You are reviewing previously generated Anki cards in CSV format.

Your goal is to create a stricter second-stage optimization pass:
maximize exam-focused memorization efficiency, reduce card count where sensible,
and preserve all central examinable concepts without preserving low-yield slide trivia.

This is NOT merely a wording cleanup pass.
This is an exam-yield compression pass.

You must preserve correctness, important definitions, formulas, distinctions, procedures,
edge cases, and common exam traps.

You may remove or absorb details that are technically correct but unlikely to be directly examined,
provided the central concept remains covered.

You must NOT:

* introduce new knowledge
* distort the meaning of the original cards
* merge unrelated concepts
* remove important examinable mechanisms, formulas, definitions, contrasts, or procedures
* handwave details that are likely to be tested

---

## PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

Convert a broad, slide-faithful Anki set into a smaller, denser, exam-focused set.

Prefer cards that cover:

* core definitions
* mechanisms and workflows
* formulas with interpretation
* important contrasts
* decision rules
* algorithmic steps
* architecture components
* exam traps
* likely calculation/evaluation concepts

Deprioritize or remove standalone cards for:

* historical trivia
* dataset names/counts unless central to the concept
* benchmark/timeline observations unless they explain an important architectural shift
* obvious consequences already implied by another card
* repeated "question answered" cards when the distinction can be merged into a concept contrast
* narrow implementation details unlikely to be examined
* examples that do not introduce a general rule

---

## CONDENSING RULES

For each card or cluster of related cards:

1. Remove:

   * redundant phrasing
   * filler words
   * conversational transitions
   * worked numeric examples unless the example itself is examinable
   * standalone low-yield context
   * duplicate consequences already implied by another card

2. Prefer:

   * compact declarative fragments
   * fewer, stronger cards
   * one coherent exam-relevant recall target per card
   * dense but readable phrasing
   * formulas paired with meaning/interpretation
   * merged concept clusters when they are normally recalled/tested together

3. Preserve:

   * precise definitions
   * central mechanisms
   * important formulas
   * variable meanings where needed
   * distinctions and contrasts
   * decision criteria
   * algorithm steps
   * common exam traps
   * practical caveats likely to be tested

---

## EXAM-YIELD MERGING RULES

You SHOULD merge cards when they form one natural exam-relevant concept cluster.

Merging is encouraged for:

1. Definition + motivation
   Example:
   "Transfer learning: definition"
   "Transfer learning: motivation"
   -> "Transfer learning: definition + motivation (2)"

2. Definition + formula + interpretation
   Example:
   "IoU definition"
   "IoU formula"
   "IoU interpretation"
   -> "Intersection over Union: definition + formula"

3. Strategy name + core procedure
   Example:
   "Feature extraction"
   "Feature extractor workflow"
   -> one compact card if the workflow is short

4. Concept + exam trap
   Example:
   "Bounding box parameterization"
   "Corner parameterization challenge"
   -> one card if the trap is tightly bound to the concept

5. Closely related contrasts
   Example:
   classification vs. localization vs. detection
   -> one contrast card if the exam target is distinguishing them

6. Algorithm purpose + essential steps
   Example:
   NMS purpose + greedy procedure
   -> one or two cards depending on density

7. Metric definition + usage caveat
   Example:
   mAP definition + IoU-threshold dependence
   -> merge if concise

You MAY merge across cards that do not use the exact same title/entity if:

* they belong to the same exam concept
* they are normally tested together
* the merged card remains one coherent recall target
* merging reduces cognitive load

You MUST NOT merge if:

* the result mixes unrelated topics
* the card becomes a grab bag
* the merge hides an important distinction
* the result becomes too long for effective recall
* the merge turns multiple independent mechanisms into one overloaded card

---

## LOW-YIELD DETAIL HANDLING

Remove or compress details that are unlikely to be examined independently.

Usually remove:

* exact dataset sizes
* exact benchmark numbers
* dates and author names unless directly tied to a core concept
* long lists of examples/applications
* source citations
* minor historical milestones
* repeated “this improved performance” statements without mechanism
* concrete examples after the general rule is already captured

Usually preserve:

* named architectures if they define a tested concept
* names of important methods/metrics/losses/algorithms
* qualitative trends that explain method design
* formulas and what each important term means
* decision rules
* structural components of architectures
* algorithm steps
* caveats that affect correctness

When unsure:

* keep the concept
* cut the prose
* merge the detail into a broader card if it supports the recall target
* do not keep a standalone card for low-yield detail

---

## ENUMERATION RULES

Use enumeration only for closed, structurally meaningful sets such as:

* algorithm steps
* fixed components
* validity conditions
* strategy options
* evaluation outcomes
* decision factors
* architecture parts
* advantages/disadvantages explicitly presented as a fixed set

If enumerated, title format MUST be:

Topic: description (N)

Backside format MUST be a single line:

1. ..., 2. ..., ..., N. ...

Do NOT enumerate loose property clusters, prose summaries, or open-ended example lists.

For dense enumerations:

* keep the list card short with item names only
* move explanations into separate cards only if each item is independently examinable
* otherwise compress each item into a short phrase

---

## FORMULA HANDLING

For formulas:

* keep the concept meaning before or with the formula
* include concise variable explanations when needed
* use inline MathJax for all math and non-ASCII symbols: \( ... \)
* avoid raw Unicode math symbols outside MathJax

Preferred style:
"Overlap between prediction \(B\) and ground truth \(B'\): \(\mathrm{IoU}(B,B')=\frac{F(B\cap B')}{F(B\cup B')}\), where \(F(X)\) is area."

Avoid formula-only cards unless the formula is self-explanatory.

---

## SPLITTING RULES

You MAY split a card if:

* it contains multiple independent recall targets
* it combines unrelated mechanisms
* it is too dense for effective recall
* one part is central and another part is low-yield

When splitting:

* keep central concepts
* remove low-yield context
* avoid creating micro-cards for obvious consequences

---

## TITLE RULES

* Title must not be a question.
* Title must not reveal the answer.
* Title must remain precise after shortening.
* Prefer:

  * "Topic: focus"
  * "Topic: definition + motivation (N)"
  * "Topic: components (N)"
  * "Topic: procedure (N)"
  * "Topic A vs. Topic B"
* If enumerated, include exact (N).
* Avoid overly narrow slide-specific titles unless the detail is exam-relevant.

---

## BACKSIDE RULES

* No full paragraphs.
* Prefer compact fragments over full prose.
* Avoid repeating title wording.
* Keep explanations concise but sufficient.
* Keep related facts together when they form one recall target.
* Use semicolons for compact property summaries.
* Use enumeration for closed checklists.
* Use inline MathJax for all math and non-ASCII symbols.

## TARGET STYLE EXAMPLES

Example 1 — definition + motivation merge:

Motivations: 1. Data requirements: good performance needs much labeled data, often expensive manual labeling. 2. Computational cost: large-dataset CNN training can take days or weeks, even with multiple GPUs."

Example 3 — formula + interpretation merge:

Before:
"Intersection over Union","Overlap measure between ground-truth and predicted bounding boxes."
"Intersection over Union: formula","\(\mathrm{IoU}(B,B')=\frac{F(B\cap B')}{F(B\cup B')}\)."
"Intersection over Union: interpretation","Range \(0\) to \(1\); higher is better."

After:
"Intersection over Union: definition + formula","Box-overlap measure between prediction \(B\) and ground truth \(B'\): \(\mathrm{IoU}(B,B')=\frac{F(B\cap B')}{F(B\cup B')}\), where \(F(X)\) is area; \(0\)=no overlap, \(1\)=perfect overlap."

Example 4 — procedure + trap merge:

Before:

"Fine-tuning: learning-rate trap","Use lower learning rate after unfreezing."

After:
"Fine-tuning procedure + trap (5)","1. Load pretrained base and replace head. 2. Freeze base. 3. Train new head. 4. Unfreeze selected later base layers. 5. Jointly train with lower learning rate, e.g. \(1/10\) or \(1/100\), to avoid destroying pretrained features."
